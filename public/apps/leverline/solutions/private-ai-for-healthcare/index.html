Private AI for Healthcare Practices

Clinicians and staff see the value of AI for summarizing records, drafting referral letters and answering policy questions, but PHI can't be pasted into consumer chatbots. A private language model deployed inside your environment, with access controls and audit logs, makes everyday AI use possible without sending health information to a third party.

What it looks like (illustrative)

A multi-specialty clinic deploys a private assistant on a server inside its network. Staff draft prior-authorization letters from chart excerpts, summarize long outside records before visits and search internal policies in plain English. Access is tied to existing roles, every prompt is logged, and drafts are always reviewed by a clinician before they're sent.

How we deliver it

  1. Classify use cases by PHI exposure and clinical risk

  2. Deploy a private model with role-based access and audit logging

  3. Connect approved sources such as policies, templates and de-identified examples

  4. Train staff on review requirements and the practice AI policy

What to plan for

  • HIPAA compliance depends on your safeguards, risk analysis and BAAs. Private hosting simplifies it but doesn't replace them.
  • Clinical decisions stay with clinicians; the assistant drafts and summarizes, it doesn't diagnose.
  • Minimum-necessary access should shape which records the assistant can retrieve.

What we measure

  • Time to draft letters and summaries
  • Staff hours saved per week
  • Policy question response time

Questions we hear

Is a private AI model HIPAA compliant?

HIPAA applies to how you handle PHI, not to a model by itself. Running the model in your environment, limiting access, logging use and documenting it in your risk analysis lets you use AI within your HIPAA program.

Can it connect to our EHR?

Often, through read-only integrations or exports, depending on your EHR. Many practices start with templates and policies, then add record summaries once safeguards are proven.

Are local models good enough compared with ChatGPT or Claude?

For most business work, such as drafting, summarizing, extracting and searching your own documents, current open-weight models are close enough that users rarely notice. For the hardest reasoning tasks we can route approved, redacted requests to a frontier API. We benchmark on your tasks before you buy any hardware.

What hardware do we need?

It depends on concurrent users and model size. Small teams can start on a single workstation-class GPU or a high-memory Apple silicon machine; larger firms typically use one or more data-center GPUs. We size it from your usage, not a catalog.
