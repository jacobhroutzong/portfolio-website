AI Demand Forecasting for Distributors & Manufacturers

Distributors live and die by inventory. Too much ties up cash and warehouse space; too little loses orders to competitors. Machine-learning forecasts that account for seasonality, customer ordering patterns and supplier lead times give buyers better reorder points for thousands of SKUs at once.

Estimate your ROI Book a call
What it looks like (illustrative)

An industrial distributor with 15,000 active SKUs across three branches replaces min/max spreadsheets with weekly forecasts. Fast movers get item-level models; the long tail is forecast by product family. Buyers see recommended order quantities with the reason behind each one (a seasonal ramp, a large customer's ordering cadence, a longer supplier lead time) and approve them in the ERP.

How we deliver it

  1. Extract sales, inventory, purchase and lead-time history from the ERP

  2. Benchmark the current method's forecast error by SKU segment

  3. Train models and set service-level targets by item class

  4. Publish weekly recommendations into purchasing with explanations

What to plan for

  • Segmenting SKUs into fast, slow and intermittent movers matters more than any single algorithm.
  • Lead-time variability often drives stockouts as much as demand does.
  • Buyers keep the final say; overrides are tracked to improve the model.

What we measure

  • Forecast error (WAPE) vs. old method
  • Fill rate
  • Inventory turns
  • Excess and obsolete stock

Questions we hear

Which ERPs do you work with?

Common mid-market ERPs such as NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics, SAP Business One, Epicor and Acumatica, typically through exports or APIs into a forecasting pipeline.

How long until we see results?

A backtest against your history shows expected accuracy within a few weeks; inventory changes show up over the next one or two replenishment cycles.

How much history do we need?

Ideally two to three years of sales by SKU and location so the model can learn seasonality. With less, we start simpler and improve as data accrues.

Will buyers trust the forecasts?

Trust comes from transparency. Every recommendation shows the drivers behind it, and we track accuracy against the old method so the numbers speak for themselves.
