The New Homeowner's Smart Home Checklist

Closing day hands you a set of keys, a garage remote and often a house full of devices tied to someone else's accounts. The first month is when small security and maintenance fixes are easiest, and when you set the habits the house will run on for years.

Illustrative scenario

A couple closes on a 1998 two-story house. In one design visit we factory reset the previous owner's video doorbell and thermostat, replace the front and garage-entry deadbolts with keypad locks, give the movers a temporary code that expires on Friday, locate the main water shutoff in a basement closet and quote an automatic valve, and test Wi-Fi in the room that will become the home office.

Checklist

  • Change or rekey every exterior lock, including the door from the garage
  • Reset the garage opener and keypad codes
  • Factory reset devices you're keeping and move them to your accounts
  • Find the main water shutoff and label it
  • Test smoke and CO alarms and note their install dates
  • Test Wi-Fi in each room you'll work, stream or sleep in
  • Replace the thermostat schedule (or the thermostat) and look up utility rebates

Worth knowing

  • Previous owners can sometimes still see cameras or doorbells until the device is removed from their account, so reset first.
  • Moving week is the best time for wiring work because rooms are empty.
  • Keep receipts and the floor plan; they help with insurance records.

Questions homeowners ask

Do I need to replace locks the previous owner left?

You need to make sure no one else can open them. Rekeying works for standard locks; smart deadbolts also let you see who comes and goes.

How long does a move-in setup take?

Most move-in packages take one day.
