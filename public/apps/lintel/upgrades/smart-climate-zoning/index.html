The problem

The thermostat sits in the hallway while everyone is in the bonus room over the garage. Schedules never get set, older systems lack the wire smart thermostats need, and one room is always too hot.

What we do

We check your HVAC wiring and add a common-wire adapter if needed, set up schedules and away modes, place room sensors where people spend time, and add smart vents to rebalance problem rooms. We enroll you in your utility's rebate or demand-response program when one exists, so the utility can pay you for small adjustments on peak days.

Questions homeowners ask

Is there still a tax credit for smart thermostats?

Not federally. The 25C energy-efficient home improvement credit ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. Many utilities still offer rebates or pay you to join demand-response programs, and we look those up for your address.

My thermostat has only two wires. Can I still get a smart one?

Usually. We add a common-wire adapter or run a new wire so the thermostat gets steady power.

What is demand response?

Your utility pays you, usually with a sign-up bonus or yearly credit, to let it nudge your thermostat a couple of degrees on a few peak days. You can always override it.
