Churn Prediction for SaaS Companies

In SaaS, churn is decided long before the renewal date: in falling logins, stalled onboarding, unresolved tickets and a champion who left. A churn model watches those signals across every account, ranks risk weekly and tells customer success exactly where to spend its time.

What it looks like (illustrative)

A B2B SaaS company with 1,200 accounts combines product usage, seat utilization, support tickets, billing events and CRM contact changes into a weekly health model. The top-risk list goes to customer success with the drivers for each account, while automated plays handle lower-value accounts: an onboarding nudge, a feature-adoption email or an invitation to a training session.

How we deliver it

  1. Define churn and contraction and assemble historical outcomes

  2. Engineer features from usage, support, billing and CRM data

  3. Train and validate risk models with per-account explanations

  4. Push scores and plays into the CRM and measure saved revenue

What to plan for

  • Product usage data is usually the strongest signal, so invest in event tracking first.
  • Explanations matter as much as scores for customer-success adoption.
  • A randomized control group proves the impact of save plays.

What we measure

  • Net revenue retention
  • Gross churn
  • Precision of the top-risk list
  • Revenue saved vs. control

Questions we hear

How early can a model predict churn?

Many SaaS businesses see meaningful risk signals 60 to 120 days before renewal, depending on contract length and how well product usage is tracked.

Do we need a data warehouse?

It helps. If your product data is already in Snowflake, Databricks or BigQuery we build there; if not, we can set up a lightweight pipeline as part of the project.

How many customers do we need for a churn model?

A few hundred historical churn events give a model enough to learn from. Smaller customer bases still benefit from a rules-based health score, which we can upgrade later.

Isn't a health score enough?

A manual health score is a good start, but it's built on assumptions. A model tests which signals actually predict churn in your data, which are often different from what the team expects.
