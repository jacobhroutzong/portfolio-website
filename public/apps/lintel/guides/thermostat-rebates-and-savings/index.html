Smart Thermostat Savings and Rebates After the Tax Credit Ended

Until the end of 2025, many homeowners counted on the federal 25C credit for efficiency upgrades. It ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. The savings from a well-set-up smart thermostat didn't end, and many utilities still pay rebates or ongoing credits.

Illustrative scenario

A household spends about $2,000 a year on heating and cooling. At the roughly 8% average savings ENERGY STAR reports for certified smart thermostats, that's about $160 a year. Their utility, in this illustrative case, also offers a one-time rebate and a yearly demand-response credit, which we enroll them in at install.

Checklist

  • Confirm the thermostat is ENERGY STAR certified (most rebates require it)
  • Check your electric and gas utility for rebates before buying
  • Ask whether your utility runs a demand-response or "bring your own thermostat" program
  • Add room sensors so the thermostat reads the rooms you use
  • Set schedules and away mode on day one; that's where savings come from

Worth knowing

  • Savings depend on climate, system type and how schedules were set before.
  • Demand-response programs adjust temperatures on a few peak days; you can override.
  • Heat pumps need thermostats configured for them to avoid using backup heat.

Questions homeowners ask

Is a smart thermostat still worth it without a tax credit?

For most homes, yes. It pays back from bill savings, often faster with a utility rebate, and the comfort benefits come with it.
