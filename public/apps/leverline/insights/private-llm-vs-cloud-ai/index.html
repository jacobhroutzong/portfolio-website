Private LLM vs. Cloud AI: A Decision Guide for Regulated Businesses

When does it make sense to run your own language model instead of using a cloud AI service? Five questions, a cost formula and three architectures for firms that handle confidential or regulated data.

By Leverline ResearchPublished October 21, 20266 min read

Short version: for most businesses handling confidential data, the answer is a hybrid. Sensitive, high-volume work runs on a private model you control; the occasional task that truly needs a frontier model goes to a cloud API, redacted and approved. Cost alone rarely justifies private AI for light chat use. Control, compliance and predictable cost at high volume usually do.

Five questions that decide it

1. What data will the AI touch? Public marketing copy carries little risk. Client files, health information, controlled unclassified information (CUI) or privileged legal material change the calculus. If a data-processing agreement or business associate agreement can't cover a cloud provider, or your clients won't accept one, private is the only option.

2. How much volume? A few dozen people chatting occasionally is cheap on any cloud API. Automations that read every invoice, call transcript or support ticket generate a lot of tokens. Hardware is a fixed cost, so private deployment gets cheaper per request as volume grows.

3. How hard are the tasks? Summarizing, extracting fields, classifying and answering questions from your own documents are well within reach of current open-weight models. The hardest multi-step reasoning still favors frontier cloud models.

4. Who will run it? A private model is a system: patching, monitoring, model updates, backups and access control. Budget for a managed service or in-house time.

5. What do clients and contracts require? Outside-counsel guidelines, defense contracts and healthcare partners increasingly specify where data may be processed. Those requirements often decide the question before cost does.

A simple cost comparison

Use your own numbers. Model prices change often.

  • Cloud monthly cost ≈ requests per month × average tokens per request × price per token
  • Private monthly cost ≈ (hardware cost ÷ 36 months) + power and hosting + support

Illustrative example: 60 staff making 40 requests a day at about 3,000 tokens each is roughly 160 million tokens a month. At a blended cloud price of $5 per million tokens, that's about $800 a month. A $25,000 GPU server amortized over three years is about $700 a month before power and support, so on cost alone, cloud wins at this volume. Add a document-processing automation that reads 50,000 pages a month, or compare against per-seat enterprise licenses, and the picture can flip.

The point isn't the exact numbers. It's to run the comparison with your real usage before buying hardware.

Three architectures

  1. Fully private (on-prem or air-gapped). Model, retrieval and interface run on your hardware with no internet connection. Best for CUI, privileged material and strict data-residency rules.
  2. Private cloud (your VPC). Open-weight models on GPU instances inside your own cloud account. Most of the control of on-prem with elastic capacity.
  3. Hybrid with a router. A private model handles sensitive and high-volume work; a routing layer sends approved, redacted requests to a frontier API. This is where most regulated mid-sized firms land.

A practical checklist

  • Classify the data each use case touches before choosing a deployment
  • Benchmark two or three models on your real tasks, not public leaderboards
  • Tie access to existing identity and log every request
  • Document the system in your security program (risk analysis, SSP or WISP)
  • Plan model updates through change control
  • Re-run the cost comparison every six months as prices and models change
