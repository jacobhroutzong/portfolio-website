Smart Upgrades Worth Making Before You List

Sellers want upgrades that photograph well, make showings easier and don't create questions at closing. That rules out elaborate systems and rules in a few familiar devices that buyers already understand.

Illustrative scenario

A seller listing in three weeks adds a video doorbell, a keypad lock for showings, a smart thermostat and dimmers in the kitchen and living room. Agents get time-limited codes for showings, and at closing we reset every device, transfer what the buyers want and leave a one-page guide.

Checklist

  • Keypad lock with time-limited codes for showing agents
  • Video doorbell for listing photos and security while the house is empty
  • A recognizable smart thermostat
  • Smart dimmers in the main rooms for evening showings
  • Leak sensors if the home will sit vacant

Worth knowing

  • Skip complex systems buyers would need training to use.
  • Note devices in the listing and confirm which ones convey with the house.
  • Reset and transfer accounts at closing, and keep a record.

Questions homeowners ask

Which upgrades should I skip?

Anything custom or subscription-heavy. Buyers value simple, familiar devices.

Do smart devices stay with the house?

Devices attached to the house, like locks, thermostats and doorbells, usually convey unless the contract says otherwise. Ask your agent and list anything you plan to take with you.

How do buyers take over the accounts?

We factory-reset each device after closing, or transfer it to the buyers' accounts if they want it set up, and leave a one-page guide with model numbers and support links.
