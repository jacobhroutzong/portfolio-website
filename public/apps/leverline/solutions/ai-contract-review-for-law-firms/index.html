AI Contract & Document Review for Law Firms

Due diligence, lease abstraction and discovery all come down to reading large volumes of documents and pulling out the same facts again and again. AI extraction turns that reading into structured tables (parties, dates, renewal terms, change-of-control and indemnity language) so attorneys spend their time on judgment instead of highlighting.

What it looks like (illustrative)

In an acquisition, a firm must review 1,800 customer contracts for assignment and change-of-control provisions. An extraction pipeline running on private models reads every agreement, fills a review table with the relevant clause text and a confidence score, and flags non-standard language. Paralegals verify the flagged items and spot-check the rest, and the partner receives a risk summary days earlier than a fully manual review would allow.

How we deliver it

  1. Define the clause and field schema with the supervising attorney

  2. Benchmark extraction accuracy on a hand-reviewed sample

  3. Run the full set with confidence scores and exception queues

  4. Deliver review tables and summaries into the firm's existing tools

What to plan for

  • Every extracted field keeps a link to the exact clause text so reviewers can verify quickly.
  • Confidence thresholds decide what a person must check. Set them conservatively at first.
  • Fixed-fee diligence benefits most, because efficiency improves margin directly.

What we measure

  • Documents reviewed per hour
  • Field-level accuracy on the sample
  • Days saved on diligence timelines

Questions we hear

How accurate is AI contract extraction?

Accuracy varies by document type and clause complexity, so we measure it on a reviewed sample from your matter before relying on it. Low-confidence results always go to a person.

Does this replace e-discovery platforms?

No. It complements them, especially for transactional review and abstraction work where traditional review tools are a poor fit.

How accurate is AI data extraction?

It depends on document quality and type, so we measure it on your documents before launch. Fields below a confidence threshold are routed to a reviewer, so the accuracy of what reaches your system is controlled rather than assumed.

Can it handle handwriting and poor scans?

Often, yes. Modern vision models handle far more than legacy OCR. Truly illegible items are flagged for review instead of guessed.
