The problem

Most churn is only noticed at cancellation. Customer success teams spread attention evenly or chase the loudest accounts, and nobody knows which early signals actually predict leaving.

How we solve it

A model trained on your customers' history scores every account weekly, highlights the drivers (falling usage, support friction, billing issues) and triggers targeted interventions, from automated nudges to a call from an account manager.

What you get

  • Churn-risk and health-score models with explanations
  • Weekly risk list in your CRM or customer-success tool
  • Automated save plays by risk segment
  • Retention experiment design and measurement
  • Executive retention dashboard

How it works

  1. Define churn

    Agree what counts as churn or contraction and assemble historical examples.

  2. Find the signals

    Engineer features from usage, billing, support and engagement data.

  3. Score and explain

    Train, validate and publish weekly scores with the top reasons per account.

  4. Act and measure

    Launch save plays and measure retained revenue against a control group.

Example (illustrative)

A SaaS company with 1,500 customers paying $6,000 a year loses 15% of them annually. Reducing that churn by a relative 15% keeps about 34 more customers a year, or roughly $203,000 in annual recurring revenue.

Questions we hear

How many customers do we need for a churn model?

A few hundred historical churn events give a model enough to learn from. Smaller customer bases still benefit from a rules-based health score, which we can upgrade later.

Isn't a health score enough?

A manual health score is a good start, but it's built on assumptions. A model tests which signals actually predict churn in your data, which are often different from what the team expects.

How do you prove the model saved revenue?

We hold back a random control group from interventions and compare retention, so you see the incremental revenue, not just a correlation.
