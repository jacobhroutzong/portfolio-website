AI Document Processing for Accounting Firms

Every tax season, skilled staff spend thousands of hours typing numbers from client documents into software. AI extraction reads W-2s, 1099s, K-1s, brokerage and bank statements and receipts, maps the values to the right fields and sends only uncertain items to a preparer, so busy-season capacity goes to review and advisory work.

What it looks like (illustrative)

A 30-person firm prepares around 2,500 individual returns. Client uploads flow through an extraction pipeline that identifies each document type, pulls the relevant figures, compares them with the prior year and flags anomalies such as a missing 1099 or a large swing in income. Preparers start each return from a pre-populated workpaper instead of a stack of PDFs.

How we deliver it

  1. Inventory document types and target fields in your tax software

  2. Build extraction and prior-year comparison rules

  3. Route low-confidence fields and anomalies to a review queue

  4. Push approved data to your tax and workpaper systems

What to plan for

  • Taxpayer data falls under the FTC Safeguards Rule and IRS security expectations, so processing can run on private models with access logging.
  • Prior-year comparison catches missing documents early, before the deadline crunch.
  • Start with the highest-volume document types and expand each season.

What we measure

  • Minutes of data entry per return
  • Returns completed per preparer
  • Errors caught in review

Questions we hear

Does this work with our tax software?

We integrate with common tax and practice-management platforms through their import formats or APIs. Where direct import isn't possible, we produce structured workpapers your staff can use.

Can clients keep uploading documents the way they do now?

Yes. We work with your existing portal or upload process and add extraction behind it.

How accurate is AI data extraction?

It depends on document quality and type, so we measure it on your documents before launch. Fields below a confidence threshold are routed to a reviewer, so the accuracy of what reaches your system is controlled rather than assumed.

Can it handle handwriting and poor scans?

Often, yes. Modern vision models handle far more than legacy OCR. Truly illegible items are flagged for review instead of guessed.
