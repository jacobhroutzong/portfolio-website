Automatic Water Shutoff Valves: Cost, Savings and Insurance Discounts

Short version: a whole-home automatic shutoff valve with leak and freeze sensors costs roughly $1,500 installed in a typical home in our price list. It's one of the few smart-home upgrades whose value comes from avoiding a very large bill, and some insurers now discount premiums for it.

Why water is the risk to plan for

State Farm reported more than 20,000 frozen-pipe claims from January 2024 through June 2025, totaling over $628 million in paid losses, with an average claim payment above $30,000 (Carrier Management, January 2026). Burst pipes are only one source; supply lines, water heaters, washers and dishwashers fail too, often when nobody is home.

How an automatic shutoff works

A valve on the main water line measures flow. When it sees a pattern that looks like a burst or a leak, or a linked sensor detects water, it closes the main line and alerts your phone. Installing the valve is plumbing work on the main line, which most areas require a licensed plumber to do.

What it costs with us

Our Safe & Dry package includes the valve, six leak sensors, two freeze sensors and a hub, installed and tested. See the current price on our packages page; older homes or hard-to-reach mains cost more.

Estimating the value honestly

There's no single right number, so use your own inputs:

  • Chance of a serious leak in a year: depends on the home's age, plumbing, climate and how often it's empty. We use 2% as an illustrative default, not a statistic.
  • Typical cost of one: your deductible plus anything not covered, and the disruption. State Farm's frozen-pipe average was above $30,000; we default to a more conservative $15,000.
  • Share prevented: a valve doesn't stop every drip, but it stops the long-running leaks that cause the biggest losses.
  • Insurance discount: varies by insurer and state, from none to a meaningful percentage.

Insurance discounts in 2026

Programs are spreading but uneven. In July 2026, Stand Insurance and Moen launched a program in California and Florida that gives premium discounts to policyholders who activate and maintain a Flo shutoff device, and waives a water endorsement requirement for some older homes (Coverager). Ask your agent what your carrier offers and what documentation it needs; we provide an installation record for insurers.

What about tax credits?

The federal 25C energy-efficient home improvement credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, and it never covered water shutoff valves. Check your water utility and insurer instead.
