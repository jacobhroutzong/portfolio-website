The problem

Every new question becomes a ticket for an already-busy analyst. Dashboards answer last quarter's questions, and self-serve BI still requires knowing where the data lives and how each metric is defined.

How we solve it

Accuracy comes from definitions, not just the model. We build a semantic layer with your metrics and business terms, connect a text-to-SQL assistant (Snowflake Cortex Analyst, Databricks Genie or an open-source stack) and test it on real questions before people rely on it.

What you get

  • Semantic model and metric definitions for core business areas
  • Natural-language assistant in Slack, Teams or your BI tool
  • Question-and-answer evaluation set with accuracy reporting
  • Row-level security matched to your existing roles
  • Usage and accuracy monitoring

How it works

  1. Collect real questions

    Gather the questions managers actually ask, with the correct answers.

  2. Define the metrics

    Build semantic views so "revenue" and "active customer" mean one thing.

  3. Connect and test

    Wire up the assistant and test until accuracy meets an agreed bar.

  4. Expand by domain

    Launch to one team, then add sales, finance or operations.

Example (illustrative)

A four-person data team spends about 44 hours a week on ad-hoc requests. Letting managers self-serve half of them returns roughly 22 analyst hours a week (about $80,000 a year at $70 an hour) for deeper work.

Questions we hear

How accurate is text-to-SQL?

Vendors publish strong benchmark numbers, but real-world accuracy depends mostly on how well your metrics are defined. That's why we build the semantic layer first and measure accuracy on your own questions.

What if the answer is wrong?

Answers show the query and data used, uncertain questions are flagged, and we review failed questions weekly to fix definitions.

Do we need Snowflake or Databricks?

No. They make it easier, but we also connect to Postgres, BigQuery, SQL Server and others.
