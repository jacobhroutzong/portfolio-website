The problem

Your people are already pasting contracts, financials and customer records into public AI tools, or they're banned from using AI at all and falling behind. Neither is acceptable when you handle confidential or regulated data, and per-seat AI licenses get expensive as usage grows.

How we solve it

We stand up an open-weight model (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma or gpt-oss class) behind a familiar chat interface, connect it to your documents with permission-aware retrieval, and wrap it with logging, access control and usage policies. Sensitive work stays on your hardware; when a task truly needs a frontier model, a routing layer can send only approved, redacted requests to a cloud API.

What you get

  • Model and hardware sizing for your users, workloads and budget
  • Deployment on-prem, in your VPC, or air-gapped (Ollama, vLLM or llama.cpp)
  • Private chat workspace with SSO, roles and audit logs
  • Document retrieval (RAG) that respects existing folder permissions
  • Acceptable-use policy, staff training and an admin runbook
  • Monitoring, model updates and support under a managed plan

How it works

  1. Classify the data

    Map which data the AI will touch and which obligations apply: client confidentiality, HIPAA, CMMC / NIST SP 800-171, ITAR or contracts.

  2. Size the stack

    Benchmark candidate models on your real tasks and pick the smallest model and hardware that meets your quality bar.

  3. Deploy and connect

    Install the runtime, chat interface and retrieval layer; wire up SSO and the document sources your team actually uses.

  4. Train and govern

    Short role-based training, a usage policy, and dashboards that show adoption, cost and quality.

Example (illustrative)

A 40-person accounting firm bans public AI over client-confidentiality concerns. A single GPU server running a mid-size open model gives every staff member a private assistant for drafting client letters, summarizing engagement files and searching firm procedures. If each person saves two hours a week at a $55 loaded cost, that's roughly $229,000 a year of capacity, against a one-time build in the low tens of thousands.

Questions we hear

Are local models good enough compared with ChatGPT or Claude?

For most business work, such as drafting, summarizing, extracting and searching your own documents, current open-weight models are close enough that users rarely notice. For the hardest reasoning tasks we can route approved, redacted requests to a frontier API. We benchmark on your tasks before you buy any hardware.

What hardware do we need?

It depends on concurrent users and model size. Small teams can start on a single workstation-class GPU or a high-memory Apple silicon machine; larger firms typically use one or more data-center GPUs. We size it from your usage, not a catalog.

Can it be fully air-gapped?

Yes. Models, embeddings, retrieval and the chat interface can all run with no internet connection; updates are delivered through your approved change process.

Is a private LLM cheaper than per-seat AI licenses?

Often, once you pass a few dozen active users or run high-volume automations, because inference cost is mostly fixed hardware. We model both options during the assessment so you can see the break-even point.
