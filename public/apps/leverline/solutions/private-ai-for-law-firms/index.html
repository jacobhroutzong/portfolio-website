Private AI for Law Firms

Attorneys want the drafting and summarizing speed of modern AI; clients and the ethics rules demand that privileged information stays protected. A private large language model gives the firm both: a ChatGPT-style assistant running on a server the firm controls, connected to matter files with the same permissions your document management system already enforces.

What it looks like (illustrative)

A 25-attorney litigation firm deploys a private assistant on a single GPU server in its own environment. Associates summarize deposition transcripts, draft discovery responses from templates and ask questions across a matter's document set, with every answer citing the source page. Nothing leaves the network, usage is logged by user and matter, and the firm's AI policy documents how output is reviewed before it reaches a client or court.

How we deliver it

  1. Map matter data, DMS permissions and ethical obligations

  2. Benchmark open-weight models on the firm's real drafting and summary tasks

  3. Deploy the model, chat workspace and matter-aware retrieval with SSO

  4. Train attorneys on verification, citation checking and the firm policy

What to plan for

  • ABA Formal Opinion 512 expects lawyers to understand AI tools, protect confidentiality and verify output. Private deployment plus training addresses all three.
  • Retrieval must respect ethical walls and matter-level permissions, not just folder access.
  • Billing practices should reflect AI efficiency; Opinion 512 cautions against billing for time not actually spent.

Questions we hear

Do law firms need a private LLM to use AI?

Not strictly. Some firms use enterprise cloud tools with strong contractual protections. A private model removes third-party exposure entirely, which many firms and their clients prefer for privileged work.

Can the assistant cite case law?

It searches and summarizes the documents you give it, but legal research still needs a citator. We connect it to your research tools where licensing allows and train users to verify every citation.

Are local models good enough compared with ChatGPT or Claude?

For most business work, such as drafting, summarizing, extracting and searching your own documents, current open-weight models are close enough that users rarely notice. For the hardest reasoning tasks we can route approved, redacted requests to a frontier API. We benchmark on your tasks before you buy any hardware.

What hardware do we need?

It depends on concurrent users and model size. Small teams can start on a single workstation-class GPU or a high-memory Apple silicon machine; larger firms typically use one or more data-center GPUs. We size it from your usage, not a catalog.
