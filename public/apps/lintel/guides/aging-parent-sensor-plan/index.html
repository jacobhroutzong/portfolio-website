A Camera-Free Sensor Plan for an Aging Parent

Families often reach for cameras first, and parents often refuse them. A sensor plan gives most of the reassurance with none of the intrusion: it knows the kitchen got used this morning and records no images.

Illustrative scenario

An 82-year-old lives alone in a one-story house; her son lives two hours away. We place motion sensors in the bedroom, hallway, bathroom and kitchen, door sensors on the front door and fridge, and motion-activated path lighting from bed to bathroom. After two weeks of learning her routine, the son gets a text only if the kitchen sees no activity by 10am or the front door opens between midnight and 5am.

Checklist

  • Agree on what's installed with the parent, in writing if helpful
  • Start with the bedroom, bathroom and kitchen
  • Add night path lighting first; lighting the route to the bathroom is standard fall-prevention advice
  • Give caregivers their own lock codes
  • Keep a medical alert device for emergencies

Worth knowing

  • Sensors don't diagnose anything; they report activity.
  • Too many alerts lead families to ignore them. We tune for few, meaningful alerts.
  • Shared accounts let more than one family member help.

Questions homeowners ask

What if my parent has pets?

We use pet-aware sensor placement and settings to avoid false activity.

Who sees the alerts?

Only the people your parent agrees to. Each family member gets their own login, and alerts can go to one person first and a second person if nobody responds.

Does it need Wi-Fi in the parent's home?

Yes, for alerts to leave the house. We add a cellular backup when the internet there is unreliable.
